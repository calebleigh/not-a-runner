# Product

## Who it's for

Adults who are out of shape, often heavier, maybe with sore knees, who don't enjoy running but want a goal on the calendar. They've tried "couch to 5K" apps and quit because running every session hurt.

## The pitch

Train for a race even if you hate running. Bike, walk and lift your way there, then add running slowly when your body is ready.

## What makes it different

- Biking and walking are first-class training, not a downgrade.
- Any cardio session can be swapped (bike, walk, walk/run). The app adjusts the time so it stays a fair workout, and warns when too many on-foot sessions get swapped away before a running race.
- Short bodyweight strength every weekday, no gym. Exercises upgrade automatically when the user marks gear they own (bands, kettlebell, foam roller).
- The plan adjusts itself from how sessions felt, pace, and heart rate.
- Steps, extra walks and hikes count toward totals without distorting the training plan.
- One clear thing to do today. No wall of text.

## Market notes (Oct 2026)

- Crowded at the generic level: Strava (owns Runna), Runna, Nike Run Club and Garmin Coach (free), and many Couch to 5K apps.
- Runna is about $19.99/month, Strava about $11.99/month.
- Social features are not a differentiator; Strava owns that. Keep social small: shareable progress cards, private challenges with a few friends.

## Business plan

1. Build it for the owner first, with onboarding that works for anyone.
2. Owner uses it for a few months.
3. Landing page and waitlist. Share in beginner running and weight-loss communities and local St. George groups. 20 to 50 signups means add payments.
4. Possible pricing: free plan generator and logging; paid tier ($5 to $8/month) for adaptive adjustments, Health Connect sync, and advanced stats. Decide later.

## Out of scope for v1

AI coach chat, social feeds, leaderboards, iOS app, payments.
